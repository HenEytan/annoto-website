For years, learning data lived inside the LMS: a course was a SCORM package, and the LMS recorded whether it was completed and what score was achieved. Learning increasingly happens elsewhere, in video platforms, simulations, mobile apps and on the job. xAPI, the Experience API, was created to capture that activity wherever it happens. It was developed under the US Advanced Distributed Learning (ADL) Initiative, originally as Project Tin Can, and version 1.0 was released in 2013. It has since been advanced as an IEEE standard.

How xAPI works

Each piece of activity is recorded as a statement with three core parts: an actor (who), a verb (did what) and an object (to what), for example "Maria completed Lecture 4" or "Sam answered Question 2 correctly". Statements can carry extra detail such as a score, a duration or a timestamp in a video. They are sent to a Learning Record Store (LRS), a database built to receive and query xAPI statements. The LRS can sit inside an LMS or stand alone, and reporting tools read from it.

What xAPI means for video

Video is one of the richest sources of learning data and one of the least used. A standard video player reports that a video was played. An engagement layer can report which segments were watched and re-watched, which questions were answered, and where learners commented or took notes. Standards such as xAPI and Caliper make it possible to bring that data into institution-wide analytics. When evaluating any video tool, ask what events it can export, in what format, and whether data can flow to your LRS or data warehouse. Frequently asked questions

Is xAPI replacing SCORM?

Not entirely. SCORM is still widely used to package and track self-paced courses in an LMS. xAPI covers what SCORM cannot, such as activity outside the LMS, offline learning and fine-grained events, and many organisations use both.

What is a Learning Record Store?

A Learning Record Store (LRS) is a system that receives, stores and returns xAPI statements. It can be built into an LMS or run as a separate service, and it is where reporting and analytics tools read xAPI data from.

Do I need xAPI if my tools support LTI 1.3?

They solve different problems. LTI 1.3 handles secure launch, roster and grade exchange with the LMS. xAPI captures detailed activity for analytics. Many institutions rely on LTI for integration and use tool exports or xAPI for deeper analysis.
