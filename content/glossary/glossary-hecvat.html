Before a university connects a new tool to its LMS and student data, its information security team needs to know how the vendor protects that data. Historically, every institution sent its own questionnaire, and vendors answered the same questions hundreds of times in slightly different forms. The HECVAT was created by higher education security professionals, and is maintained through EDUCAUSE, to standardise that process: one questionnaire, understood across the sector, that a vendor can complete once and share with many institutions.

What the HECVAT covers

The questionnaire covers company and product information, data handling and storage, encryption, authentication and access control, application security, incident response, business continuity, third-party subprocessors, and policies and compliance. It has included sections on privacy and on accessibility, and later versions consolidated earlier variants into a single, updated toolkit. Earlier versions were known as HECVAT Full and HECVAT Lite, with Lite used for lower-risk tools.

Tips for faster reviews

For institutions: ask for the HECVAT, VPAT and data processing terms at the same time, early in the process, so security, accessibility and legal reviews run in parallel rather than one after another. For evaluators comparing video tools: look closely at data residency, subprocessors, single sign-on support and how learner data is deleted at the end of a contract.

Frequently asked questions

Who maintains the HECVAT?

The HECVAT is maintained through EDUCAUSE by a community of higher education information security and privacy professionals, and is updated periodically.

Is the HECVAT a certification?

No. It is a self-reported questionnaire. Institutions often ask for supporting evidence, such as third-party audit reports or ISO 27001 certification, alongside it.

Do K-12 schools use the HECVAT?

It was designed for higher education, but some K-12 districts and other organisations accept it because it is comprehensive and widely recognised by vendors.
