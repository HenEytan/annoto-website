In a large course, one instructor cannot give detailed feedback on every draft, rehearsal and practice video. Peer assessment spreads the work: learners review each other's submissions using the same criteria the instructor will use. The benefit is not only scale. Applying a rubric to someone else's work forces the reviewer to understand what good looks like, which improves their own work.

What the research says

Reviews of the research, including Topping's widely cited 1998 review, found that peer assessment can be reliable and can improve learning, particularly when criteria are clear and learners are trained to use them. Later meta-analyses report positive effects on performance compared with no assessment or teacher-only assessment. Studies also consistently find that giving feedback is at least as valuable for learning as receiving it.

Making peer assessment fair and useful

The most common complaints are vague feedback ("great job!") and concerns about fairness. Both are design problems. A rubric with a small number of specific, observable criteria gives reviewers something concrete to comment on. Anonymity can reduce social pressure. Assigning each submission to several reviewers smooths out individual bias. And grading the quality of the feedback given, not just the work submitted, tells learners that reviewing is part of the assignment.

Peer assessment on video

Video submissions, such as presentations, language practice, clinical skills or teaching demonstrations, are well suited to peer assessment, but general comments at the end of a video are hard to act on. Timestamped feedback solves that: the reviewer comments at the exact moment the issue occurs, and the author can jump straight to it. Annoto's peer review combines rubric criteria with comments anchored to the timeline, and faculty at Washington University in St. Louis have used it to make peer feedback more specific.

Frequently asked questions

Is peer assessment the same as peer review?

The terms are often used interchangeably. Peer review usually emphasises formative feedback on drafts; peer assessment can also include scoring that contributes to a grade.

Should peer scores count toward the final grade?

Many instructors use peer feedback formatively and keep final grading with the instructor, or give peer scores a small weight. Grading the quality of reviews learners give is a common way to reward effort.

How many peer reviewers should each submission have?

Two to four reviewers per submission is a common range. Several reviewers average out individual bias and give the author a range of perspectives without overloading reviewers.
