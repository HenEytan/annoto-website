A final exam tells you what a learner knew at the end. By then it is too late to teach them differently. Formative assessment moves the check earlier: a question in the middle of a lecture, a short reflection after a reading, a draft reviewed by peers before the final submission. The defining feature is not the format but the use. An assessment is formative when its results change what the instructor or the learner does next.

Formative versus summative

Summative assessment measures achievement at the end of a unit and usually counts toward a grade. Formative assessment happens during the unit, is low-stakes or ungraded, and exists to generate feedback. The same question can be either: a multiple-choice item on a final exam is summative, the same item placed inside a lecture video with an explanation for each option is formative. Most well-designed courses use both, with frequent formative checks feeding into fewer summative ones.

What the research says

Black and Wiliam's influential 1998 review, Inside the Black Box, brought together a large body of studies and concluded that strengthening formative assessment produces significant learning gains, with some of the largest effects for lower-achieving students. Later work has emphasised that the benefit depends on feedback quality: feedback that tells learners what to do next helps; a bare score does much less.

Frequently asked questions

Should formative assessments be graded?

Usually lightly or not at all. Grading for completion rather than correctness keeps participation high while preserving the low-stakes character that makes learners willing to reveal what they do not understand.

What are examples of formative assessment in online courses?

In-video questions, reflection prompts, discussion posts anchored to specific moments in a video, practice quizzes, peer review of drafts and short exit questions at the end of a module are all common examples.

How is formative assessment different from retrieval practice?

They overlap. Retrieval practice is a learning technique that strengthens memory through recall; formative assessment is a teaching process that uses evidence of understanding to adjust instruction. An in-video question often does both at once.
