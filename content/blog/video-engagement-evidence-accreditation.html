Accreditation reviews, program reviews and quality frameworks all ask a version of the same question: how do you know students are engaging and learning? For courses built heavily on video, the honest answer is often "we know they pressed play". That is changing. When video includes questions, discussion and instructor interaction, the resulting data can document engagement and interaction far more directly than view counts or syllabus statements.

What reviewers are looking for

Requirements vary by accreditor, country and discipline, but several themes recur across reviews of online and blended programs:

  • Interaction: evidence that students interact with instructors, with each other and with content. In the US, distance education must include regular and substantive interaction initiated by instructors.
  • Assessment of learning outcomes: evidence that outcomes are measured and that results inform improvement.
  • Course design quality: alignment of activities with objectives, often reviewed against a rubric such as Quality Matters.
  • Student support and early intervention: evidence that struggling students are identified and helped.

Collect it with review in mind

Data collected casually is hard to use later. A few decisions at the start of a term make it far more useful at review time:

  1. Tag questions to outcomes. When each in-video question is linked to a course objective, question results become outcome evidence.
  2. Keep instructor interaction in the platform. Replies and feedback given on the video timeline are logged with dates. Feedback given in private email is not.
  3. Export at the end of every term. Do not rely on reconstructing data years later. A term-end export, stored with course records, makes the next review much easier. Annoto supports analytics export and API access.
  4. Record what you changed. A short note of "we re-recorded the week 4 explanation after 60 percent missed question 2" is exactly the continuous-improvement evidence reviewers look for.

Be careful with privacy

Aggregate, program-level data is usually what a review needs. Share aggregate figures and anonymised examples rather than identifiable student records, and follow institutional policies under FERPA, GDPR or local equivalents. Make sure your tools' data processing terms cover this use.

A note on RSI

For US institutions offering distance education, documenting regular and substantive interaction is a compliance matter with financial aid implications, not just a quality indicator. Timestamped instructor interaction on course video is one of the clearest records available.
