Institutions rarely regret buying a teaching tool because it did not work. They regret it because nobody used it. Licences sit idle, a handful of enthusiasts use the tool heavily, and the renewal conversation becomes awkward. Adoption is a project in its own right, and the first month matters most. This plan is written for instructional designers, teaching and learning centres and LMS administrators rolling out a new tool, but it applies to most classroom technology.

Before day 1: decide what success looks like

Pick two or three measures before launch, not after. For example: number of courses using the tool by week 8, percentage of students active in those courses, and one outcome measure such as participation or completion in pilot courses. Agree them with the sponsor so the rollout is judged on the same terms it was planned on.

Days 1 to 7: set up and recruit champions

  • Integrate properly. Install through LTI 1.3 so the tool appears inside the LMS with single sign-on and gradebook sync. Every extra login or copy-paste step costs adoption.
  • Recruit five to ten champions. Look for instructors who teach large or online courses, have a clear pain point, and are respected by colleagues. Aim for a spread of departments.
  • Pick one use case each. Each champion starts with one concrete use, such as questions in one lecture video or peer review on one assignment. Not the whole feature list.

Days 8 to 14: first use in real courses

  • Do it with them. Offer 30-minute one-to-one sessions where a designer sets up the first activity with the instructor, in their actual course.
  • Remove the blank page. Provide templates: a set of discussion prompts, a sample rubric, a question bank. AI drafting of questions from existing videos also helps instructors get started quickly.
  • Prepare student guidance. A two-minute video or one-page guide for students avoids the same support question 200 times.

Days 15 to 21: support and quick feedback

  • Check in early. A short message to each champion after their first week catches problems before they become reasons to stop.
  • Look at the data together. Showing an instructor which question most students missed, or which moment drew the most comments, is often the moment the tool becomes theirs.
  • Collect one quote and one number from each champion. You will need them in week four.

Days 22 to 30: share and expand

  • Run a show-and-tell. Champions present what they did in ten minutes each, to colleagues in their own departments. Peer examples persuade faculty far more than vendor demos.
  • Publish short case notes. A paragraph, a screenshot and one result per champion, on your teaching and learning site.
  • Open the next wave. Offer drop-in sessions and a self-service guide for the next group, with champions as department contacts.
